Fire Pit Design and Installation in Sevier County

We design and build fire pits all over Sevier County, from a stone ring you burn firewood in to a gas fire feature piped into the house. Most of the job is the part you never see: level ground, a compacted base, and on a hillside lot, often a small wall to make a flat place for it at all. We’re based in Sevierville, we’re insured, and estimates are free.

Custom fire pits, four ways to fuel one

Before we talk about stone, we ask how you’ll use it. How many people sit around it? Do you want to haul firewood, or turn a knob? Is there natural gas at the house? Those answers usually point to one of these:

  1. Wood-burning fire pit: a built-in ring of stone or block that you stack and burn wood in. It’s the simplest to build and the only one with the crackle and the smoke smell. It also needs the most room around it, away from the house, low branches and anything a spark could catch.
  2. Propane fire pit: runs off a refillable tank, so there’s no gas line to run. You get a flame when you want one, and there’s no ash to shovel out the next morning.
  3. Natural gas fire pit: plumbed into the house supply, so it won’t run out halfway through the evening. A certified gas installer we bring in runs the line, so it goes in to code.
  4. Gel fire pit: burns cans of gel fuel. No line, no tank, very little smoke. It suits a small modern bowl, and it’s the option to ask about for an indoor fire feature.

Not sure which one fits? That’s what the first visit is for. We’ll stand on the spot with you, look at what’s around it, and talk the options through before anything gets drawn up.

Why build your fire pit with Mountain Landscapers

We build the ground it sits on. A fire pit that tilts, cracks or sits in a puddle almost always failed underneath. Our own crew clears the spot, levels it, compacts the base and sets rebar in the footing of a built-in pit, so the part you can’t see is ours to answer for.

Stone, brick or block that suits the yard. For a traditional pit you can choose from a range of stones, bricks and block. We’ll help you pick something that sits right next to your patio or walls and holds up to East Tennessee winters.

Placed with safety in mind. The obvious rules get followed. We also check the things that get skipped: how far the pit sits from the house and from overhanging limbs, what’s within reach of a spark, and whether a gas feature has the ventilation it needs.

Gas and power by licensed trades. Gas lines are run by a certified gas installer and any wiring by licensed electricians. We schedule them as part of the job. If the work needs an engineer or a permit, it gets one.

Room to add on later. A fire pit is often the first piece of something bigger: a patio, seating walls, lighting. Tell us where you’d like to end up, even if you’re only building the pit this year, and we’ll set the grade so the later pieces fit.

Our Fire Pit Design and Installation Process

Here’s how a fire pit job runs with us, from the first call to the first fire.

A wood-burning pit on flat ground is a short project. A gas feature, or a pit on a slope, has more going on in the middle: the gas line, and sometimes a wall to level the ground first.

  1. Initial consultation: we talk through how you’ll use the space, which fuel you want, the size, and the materials you like.
  2. Site assessment: we look at the slope, where water runs when it rains, the branches overhead and how far the spot is from the house. Problems get found here, not halfway through the build.
  3. Customized design: we lay out the pit and whatever goes around it, matched to the patio, walls or stone you already have, with the seasons and tree clearance in mind.
  4. Materials: stone, block and brick come from local suppliers we trust, picked for color and texture and for how they handle East Tennessee’s freeze and thaw.
  5. Installation and care: we clear and level the ground, compact the base, set the rebar and build the pit. Before we leave, we’ll show you how to look after it.

Gas or wood: which fire pit should you build?

Gas is the request we get most, and it’s the easier one to live with. Turn it on, turn it off, nothing to clean up. Wood gives you the real fire and the smell of it, and there’s no gas line to plan around.

If the house already has natural gas, a pit plumbed off that supply is usually the tidiest answer. If it doesn’t, propane does the same job from a tank.

Building one for a rental cabin? Think about who’ll be lighting it. A lot of cabins around Pigeon Forge and Gatlinburg rent, and a gas fire feature that turns on with a switch is simpler to hand over to guests than a woodpile.

Fire pits on a hillside lot

Plenty of yards around here don’t have a flat spot to put a fire pit. On those lots the pit is the easy part. The real work is making level ground: a retaining wall, compacted fill, and drainage so the water coming down the slope doesn’t wash the base out the first spring.

We do the earth, the wall, the concrete and the hardscape ourselves, so one crew answers for all of it. On a mountain lot that matters, because a fire pit is only as level as the wall holding up the ground under it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1How much does it cost to have a fire pit installed?

It depends mostly on the fuel and the ground. A wood-burning pit on a flat yard is the smallest job. A gas pit adds a gas line run by a certified installer, and a pit on a slope may need a retaining wall before anything else. We price each one after seeing the spot, and the estimate is free.

Q2Do you install gas fire pits?

Yes, and gas is the request we get most. We build the pit and everything around it, and a certified gas installer we bring in runs the line so it goes in to code. Propane is the option where the house has no natural gas.

Q3Can you build a fire pit on a sloped yard?

Yes. Most of the work is making level ground first, usually with a retaining wall, compacted fill and drainage so runoff doesn’t undermine the base. We build the wall and the pit ourselves.

Q4Will you build the patio or seating around the fire pit too?

Yes. We lay paver, flagstone and concrete patios with our own crew, and build seating walls, so the pit and the space around it come from one company.

Q5Where do you build fire pits?

Across Sevier County: Sevierville, Pigeon Forge, Gatlinburg, Wears Valley, Kodak, Seymour, Boyds Creek and Pittman Center, and up into Knoxville.
