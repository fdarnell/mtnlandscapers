Water Going Where It Shouldn’t

After a hard East Tennessee storm, some yards drain and some don’t. If yours holds water for days, runs across the driveway, or pools against the foundation, that isn’t bad luck — it’s grading and runoff, and both can be fixed.

Mountain Landscapers installs yard drainage across Sevierville, Pigeon Forge, Gatlinburg, Seymour and Kodak. We’re insured, estimates are free, and we’ll tell you honestly whether you need a full drainage system or just a better downspout run.

Why East Tennessee Yards Flood

Two things make drainage harder here than in flatter parts of the country. The clay in our soil holds water instead of letting it soak through, so a yard can stay saturated for days after the rain stops. And the grades around the foothills move water fast — a storm that’s a nuisance on level ground arrives at the bottom of a slope with real force.

Add a freeze-thaw winter and standing water stops being cosmetic. It heaves walkways, undermines slabs, and turns a low spot into ice.

Signs You Have a Drainage Problem

  • Water standing more than 24 hours after rain
  • Soggy ground or moss in the same spot year-round
  • Water pooling against the foundation or running toward the house
  • Gutters and downspouts dumping right at the wall
  • Bare channels or exposed roots where runoff has cut a path
  • Mulch or gravel washing out of beds after every storm
  • A crawl space that smells damp

What We Install

  • French drains — buried perforated pipe in gravel that intercepts water underground and carries it somewhere safe
  • Catch basins and area drains — for a specific low spot that collects water
  • Channel drains — across driveways and walkways where runoff crosses hard surfaces
  • Downspout extensions and buried leaders — moving roof water away from the foundation instead of dropping it at the wall
  • Regrading and swales — reshaping the ground so water leaves on its own

We install all five, and we’ve done all five. Most yards need a combination, and we’d rather solve it with grading where grading will do it than sell you pipe you don’t need.

A Condominium in Gatlinburg That Flooded Every Time It Rained

One of the buildings took on water in every storm. The cause wasn’t the building — it was the parking lot above it. Runoff came off that asphalt, ran downhill, and arrived at the lower building with nowhere else to go.

We excavated and installed a double-piped French drain: one run tied into the building’s downspouts so roof water was captured before it reached the ground, and a second run to carry the surface water off and away.

While we were in there we found an existing drain nobody was using. It was buried at both ends and doing nothing. We cleaned it out and reopened both ends so it could carry water again — a drain that was already paid for, put back to work.

We worked alongside the property’s engineer throughout, so the installation went in according to the plan.

That’s the range: the same crew that trenches a back-yard French drain also plans and installs drainage at condominium scale. Plenty of landscapers stop well short of a multi-building site. Plenty of site contractors won’t return a call about a back yard. We do both.

We Diagnose Before We Dig

We do our own drainage consulting, so the first thing that happens is not a quote for pipe — it’s working out where the water is coming from. The wet spot you can see is usually the end of a path that starts at a roofline, a neighbor’s grade, or a low point nobody planned. The Gatlinburg job is a good example: the water showed up at a building, but the problem was a parking lot.

If your situation needs something outside what we install, we’ll say so and point you toward someone we trust for that part of the water work.

What Your Yard Looks Like When We’re Done

This is the question most people are actually asking, and the honest answer is that it depends on what you want and what you want to spend.

Sometimes the right finish is seed and hay. Sometimes it’s sod, when you’d rather not wait. Sometimes it’s decorative stone, where a planted finish doesn’t make sense or you want the drainage line to be part of the look.

We work that out with you before we start — what your budget is, and what the finished result should look like for that money. The goal is a yard that both works and looks the way you wanted, not a repaired trench.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a French drain, or would regrading fix it?

Often regrading alone will do it, and it’s the simpler solution when the ground cooperates. We’ll look at where the water is coming from and tell you which one your yard actually needs.

Will you tear up my whole lawn?

No. Drainage work is trenched along the path the water needs to take, not across the whole yard, and we finish the disturbed ground the way you’ve chosen — seed and hay, sod, or decorative stone.

Do you take on large or commercial drainage jobs?

Yes. We have planned and installed drainage for condominium properties and worked alongside engineers on projects that needed one, as well as everyday back-yard drainage.

What does yard drainage cost?

It depends on the size of the problem and the finish you want. Estimates are free — we’ll come look at it and tell you.
